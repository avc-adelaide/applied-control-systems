\begin{frame}[fragile]
\frametitle{Predator-prey model}
In previous workshops you have seen how to simulate and plot a dynamical system. Let us start by recreating Figure 4.20 from the notes: simulated results for an uncontrolled predator-prey system.

This system is governed by the two differential equations
\[\frac{\mathrm{d}H}{\mathrm{d}t} = (r+u)H\left(1-\frac{H}{k} \right)-\frac{aHL}{c+H}, \quad \frac{\mathrm{d}L}{\mathrm{d}t} = \frac{baHL}{c+H}-dL.\]  
The variables are  
\begin{itemize}
	\item  $H$: Hare population 
	\item $L$: Lynx population 
		\item $u$: Controlled food source for hares
		
\end{itemize}
The parameters are
\begin{itemize}
	\item $a$: Rate that hare population diminishes as a function of lynx population
	\item $b$: Growth rate of lynxes
	\item $c$: Prey consumption rate for low hare population
	\item $d$: Mortality rate of lynxes
	\item $k$: Maximum population of hares (in the absence of lynxes)
	\item $r$: Growth rate of hares
\end{itemize}
\end{frame}

\begin{frame}
\frametitle{Linearising}
Taking the number of lynxes $L$ as the output, and linearising around the equilibrium point (as we have done in the previous workshop) we get
\[\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}t}\begin{bmatrix}
	z_1 \\ z_2
\end{bmatrix} = \begin{bmatrix}
	0.13 & -0.93 \\ 0.57 & 0
\end{bmatrix} \begin{bmatrix}
	z_1 \\ z_2
\end{bmatrix}  + \begin{bmatrix}
	17.2 \\ 0
\end{bmatrix} v\]
\[w=\begin{bmatrix}
	0& 1
\end{bmatrix}  \begin{bmatrix}
	z_1 \\ z_2
\end{bmatrix} \]
where $z_1=H-H_e$, $z_2=L-L_e$, and $v=u$

Putting this into Matlab, we can set up our linearised state space representation.
